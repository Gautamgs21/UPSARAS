How this engine works & assumptions worth checking
  • FIFO principal allocation. Every payment first clears the oldest unpaid quarterly principal (Q1 → Q2 → Q3 → Q4) before a rupee of it is applied to any accrued interest.
  • Inclusive month count. The number of months between a due date and a payment date is the count of distinct calendar months the period touches, regardless of the exact day — e.g. 16 July to 14 October is treated as 4 months (July, August, September, October).
  • Interest formula (monthly compounding, default): Interest = Balance × ((1 + Rate/12)^Months − 1). Annual compounding uses ((1 + Rate)^(Months/12) − 1).
  • Interest becomes new principal. Any interest generated that isn't paid off immediately is carried forward as its own liability, starting on the 1st of the month following the month in which it was generated — and it can itself generate further interest if it stays unpaid.
  • Rate / method schedule. The default rate and compounding method apply unless an effective-month change is entered. Each change remains active until the next change, so the engine can move automatically from one statutory rate/method to another without manual recalculation.
  • Moratorium vs. non-moratorium. With the moratorium checkbox ON, every rate/method change cuts through every liability — parent and every child interest/interest-on-interest — at that change's effective date, splitting any period that straddles it. With it OFF (default), a rate/method change only binds a liability chain (the quarterly SUC due and everything that compounds from it) if that chain's root due date falls on or after the change's effective date; a qualifying chain then uses that one rate/method for its entire life, and a non-qualifying chain keeps the old rate/method throughout, even for legs calculated long after the change.
  • Payment waterfall. Once all four quarters' principal is exhausted, remaining payment is applied to outstanding interest liabilities in the order they arose. Anything left after every liability is settled shows as surplus/unallocated.
  • Outstanding balances at your "as on" date get one final interest accrual shown in red — this amount is unpaid and included in the net figure, but doesn't itself compound further.
  • Excel export. The workbook mirrors your template: payment register in rows 7–15 (expands automatically if you have more payments), the four quarterly blocks in the calculation ledger, and the same total rows at the bottom — including the =E20+E40+E60+E80 style principal formula, the payment total, and the "Total Principal Due including Interest upto …" line. The template's =SUM(M37:N99) has been corrected to sum the whole interest column (M20:M100 equivalent), since the original range missed the top of the ledger.
  • Sidebar. The three input cards live in a single collapsible sidebar. Use the ‹ Hide inputs bar at the top of the sidebar to collapse it to a thin strip — click › Inputs to bring it back. Each card can also be collapsed individually with the chevron in its header. Both states are remembered in your browser.
  • Fresh start. The Fresh start button in the masthead deletes everything this app has saved in your browser (inputs, collapse states, sidebar state) and returns you to a completely blank form.
